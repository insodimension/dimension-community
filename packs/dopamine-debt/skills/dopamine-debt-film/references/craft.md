# Craft: what reviewers approved, what they rejected, and why

The person's reaction to the render is the only verdict. A film that scored near 99 on every number was still rejected as boring.

## What an approved film did
- **Real moment first.** The subject's own clip saying the quiet part plays within 20 s.
- **Every meme is the punchline of a line written for it,** about the same fact. The line sets up a number or a claim; the meme reacts to exactly that.
- **The narrator has a running flaw,** a small self-own that returns as a callback.
- **Planned music:** two cues, the bed at about −33 LUFS, ducking 9 dB under memes, and a switch at act 3.
- **Its defect, which the reviewer called out:** narration line ends cut on live speech, many right before a meme, because they were cut at timestamps instead of in silence.

## What was rejected, and the lesson from each
| What the reviewer saw | Lesson |
|---|---|
| One full-frame meme cut-away at a time, with no flow | Mix the meme modes; place each overlay on the named thing |
| A meme cut before its word; silent "step one" cards; stock robot imagery | A meme plays whole; no dead air; no "step one", no "robot" |
| A film rejected despite scores near 99 | Numbers never certify a film |
| "Completely broken": dozens of memes interrupting the narration, words cut off, overused, not fitting | Narration cut only in silence; memes stop narration only at a sentence end; no meme quotas |
| "Just saying the word is not a meme" | The meme plays its whole spoken line, setup included |
| "Bored till the first minute… no memes, no good content" | Memes with no written jokes leave a dead film; open on a real moment and write jokes |
| "Fewer jokes didn't mean one every five seconds" | Laughs go where explanation piles up, never on a clock |
| One still held while the narration talked; a number highlighted for more than three to five seconds; names stamped on a screen held for ten seconds; a blank stage for two or three seconds; the narrator explaining that a line was the joke | **Show, don't tell:** the picture changes at least every 5 s; a sticker is not a change; a receipt holds 3–4 s, then something explains it. **No blank stage.** **Act the analogy out.** **Deadpan never labels its own joke.** |
| A celebratory meme over a company's huge margin ("why would the viewer say nice? For us it's not nice"); pointing and double-take memes that landed on nothing; an animal standing in for regulators | **A meme voices the audience's feeling, not the subject's.** **Use the canonical clip.** **Every meme lands something set up.** **A picture depicts the fact; no riddle metaphors.** |
| A whole film nobody could follow: no information came out, almost no memes, a generic corporate bed, a personal claim the narrator made up | **Understood first:** pick three facts and land each with a scene before joking. **Match the surface of an approved film** (its memes, stickers, captured pages and subject cutouts in the first three minutes). **The narrator never invents personal facts.** **Draw the money loop when companies are investor, customer and seller at once.** |
| A bed made from a `motion_score` render, rejected outright | **Never ship a `motion_score` render as a bed.** Make the film's own bed or reuse an approved one; the person's ear decides. |

## Openings
- The thesis joke carrying the title nouns lands by 5 s. A real moment (a clip, a news read, a post) comes within 20 s.
- Never spend the first minute explaining the form (what a filing is). Put the form inside a joke, or later.

## Memes
- A topic audience rarely laughs at memes dropped in as decoration, but does laugh at the subject's own words and numbers played straight. A meme lands a fact; it never decorates one.
- Choose by what the line needs, then measure the clip's whole sentence. Never choose by the library's one-word `payoff.line`.

## Notes from the person
Fix exactly what each note asks, in one render. When a note hints at a flow problem (sound wanted under a silent post), fix it at its cause in that line, picture or meme.
